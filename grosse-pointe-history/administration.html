Central Office & the Administrators

The administration churn

Between 2023 and 2026, Grosse Pointe cycled through superintendents, principals and central-office leaders at a rate that alarmed staff and parents. This is the paper trail: who left, who arrived, what a board investigation found — and how the budget did the cutting.

The retaliation report — finally released

In August 2023, two Grosse Pointe North High School administrators (identified in reporting by the surnames Murray and Davis) filed a complaint alleging that board president Ahmed Ismail had told a third party of a plan or desire to "push out" the two women, and that he retaliated after they complained.

The district hired attorney Kevin Sutton of the Miller Johnson firm to investigate. His January 4, 2024 executive summary concluded that Ismail did make the comment and did engage in retaliatory behavior against the two administrators after their complaint — but that his conduct did not rise to harassment or gender bias. (The Detroit News; North Pointe Now)

The old board majority kept the full report private. It became a campaign issue: Ismail and Lisa Papas did not seek reelection in November 2024, and four progressive-leaning candidates won in part on a pledge to release it. On February 10–11, 2025, the new board voted 4–2 to release the full 27-page report. (The Detroit News; The Tower Pulse)

This is the documented core of the "driven out" story around Superintendent Jon Dean's exit — see The Schools Saga.

The North administrator exodus

The two complaints weren't isolated. By the summer of 2024, Grosse Pointe North had an almost entirely new administrative team — four new administrators taking over after a wave of resignations. (The Detroit News; Detroit Free Press, via Yahoo)

Turnover on that scale, at a single high school, in a single year, is not normal — and it happened at the school at the center of both the retaliation complaint and the halted health clinic.

Cotton's budget — and the gutting of central office

The mechanism for much of the central-office loss was the budget. With Sean Cotton serving as board treasurer, the board faced a roughly $4 million gap. According to Mary Anne Brush's federal lawsuit and contemporaneous reporting, the administration presented its recommended budget — and the board majority (Papas, Cotton, Jeup, Ismail) rejected it and substituted its own set of cuts, which fell heavily on central-office positions. (Brush lawsuit filing; Grosse Pointe News)

The result, staff and parents said, was a bare-bones central administration — a district office cut below what was needed to run a system of 6,500 students, at the same time it was losing its superintendent.

Framing: that the board substituted its own budget for the administration's recommendation is the documented fact. Whether the cuts were prudent belt-tightening or a targeted "gutting" is the contested question — this page lays out which positions disappeared so readers can judge.

Who left central office

The people behind the org chart, and where they went:

  • Jon Dean, superintendent — "retirement" accepted 4–2 in August 2023. (details)
  • Dan Hartley, director of secondary instruction — left to become principal at Bloomfield Hills High School. (C&G Newspapers)
  • Stefanie Hayes, director of special education / student services — left for Ferndale Public Schools. (C&G Newspapers)
  • Keith Howell — a GPPSS administrator since 2007 who ended up running both elementary and secondary instruction as executive director of preK-12 teaching and learning, while also coordinating the district's Title I–IV grant funding. He left in 2024 to become superintendent of L'Anse Creuse Public Schools. (Grosse Pointe News)
  • Mary Anne Brush, communications director — position eliminated in the board's budget; the communications office went from three people to one. She is suing. (details)
⚠ Needs local source  Community accounts hold that the board specifically declined to renew the contracts of Hartley (secondary) and the head of student services, and that at one stretch Keith Howell was effectively doing two jobs plus grants. The departures and destinations above are reported; the "contract not renewed" characterization and the "3-to-1" communications figure trace to the lawsuit and to board meetings, and would be strongest linked to the specific board minutes.

Superintendent Andrea Tuttle

After interim superintendent Christian Fenton, the board voted 7–0 on December 12, 2023 to hire Andrea Tuttle, who started February 5, 2024. Her salary was $310,000 with 2% annual raises — among the highest in the district's history — and in November 2024 the board extended her contract from three years to five. (C&G Newspapers; Grosse Pointe News)

The CFO win. To her credit, Tuttle filled a gap the district badly needed: in August 2024 the board unanimously hired Andrea Agrusa (from Fraser Public Schools) as chief financial officer, a role Tuttle said took weeks of persuading to fill. (Grosse Pointe News, Aug. 2024)

She also spoke repeatedly, at board meetings, about rebuilding a central office that had been hollowed out — an acknowledgment, from the top, of how thin the administration had become.

The friction. Tuttle's tenure was short and contentious. Local coverage documented tense board meetings during her time. (Grosse Pointe News) She had also left a prior superintendency, at Owosso Public Schools, on an early exit. (Argus-Press) On January 28, 2026 — barely two years in — Tuttle announced she would retire, effective August 31, 2026. (Grosse Pointe News; C&G Newspapers)

⚠ Needs local source  Community accounts describe specific incidents — that Tuttle once walked out of a meeting, and on another occasion berated trustee Valarie St. John. Those are characterizations of conduct; before stating them as fact the site should cite the specific meeting record, video, or contemporaneous report. The salary, dates, contract extension, CFO hire and retirement above are documented.

The search — and a historic hire

Tuttle's retirement sent the board back into a superintendent search barely two years after the last one. In March 2026, the board approved hiring the district's first Black superintendent — a milestone that nonetheless arrived "amid controversy," per reporting. (The Detroit News; Deadline Detroit)

The principal churn — Parcells and North

The instability reached the buildings, especially Parcells Middle School and Grosse Pointe North:

  • Sara Delgado — a 12-year GPPSS veteran (Spanish teacher, then Kerby Elementary principal) who left the district and returned as Parcells principal on August 1, 2023, then was moved to principal of Grosse Pointe North in June 2024. After fewer than two years at North, she was moved again — to a central-office role, executive director of teaching and learning, effective July 1, 2026. (Grosse Pointe News; The Tower Pulse)
  • Jason Wesley — named Parcells principal in 2024, then abruptly resigned in March 2025. (Grosse Pointe News)
  • Amanda Schmittdiel — named the next Parcells principal in June 2025, the school's third leader in roughly two years.

Three principals at one middle school inside two years — with the North principalship changing hands in the same window — is the kind of churn that shows up in a documented history precisely because families felt it directly.

Instruments while cutting

One juxtaposition residents raise, stated plainly: during the same window that the Cotton-led board was cutting the district's own budget, Sean Cotton's STEAM Foundation stepped in to buy band instruments — an amount residents put at roughly $100,000. A district cutting its core budget while a board member's affiliated foundation funds a visible extra is exactly the kind of overlap examined on the Cotton Era page.

⚠ Needs local source  The STEAM Foundation instrument purchase and the ~$100,000 figure are community accounts here; they need a primary source (the foundation's records, a board acceptance of the gift in the minutes, or news coverage). Presented as an allegation pending documentation, not asserted as fact.
