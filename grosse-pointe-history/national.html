Where the Pointes touch the national story

Some of the same names that shaped Grosse Pointe's local fights also appear in the national story of the 2020 election and its aftermath. These threads are documented in national reporting and court records — and they matter because they explain who some of the local players are.

Monica Palmer and the Wayne County vote certification

Monica Palmer, a Grosse Pointe Woods Republican, chaired the Wayne County Board of Canvassers in November 2020. In one of the most closely watched moments of the post-election period, she and fellow Republican William Hartmann first blocked certification of the county's results, then reversed and voted to certify (conditioned on an audit), then declined to sign the paperwork and tried the next day to rescind their certification votes via affidavit. (The Detroit News)

Palmer and Hartmann acknowledged receiving a phone call from President Donald Trump and RNC chair Ronna McDaniel around that reversal. Years later, a recording surfaced in which Trump is heard pressuring the two canvassers not to sign, telling them they'd look "terrible" if they did. (NBC News; Detroit News; Axios)

Local relevance: a nationally significant election-certification fight had, at its center, a Grosse Pointe resident. Palmer's later local activity (below) is why this national thread belongs in a Grosse Pointe history.

Joe Oltmann, FEC United, and the Dominion defamation case

Joe Oltmann (sometimes rendered "Joel" or "Joe Altman" in local retellings) is a Colorado podcaster and activist who founded FEC United. In November 2020 he claimed to have secretly heard a Dominion Voting Systems employee, Eric Coomer, describe rigging the election on an "antifa" call — a claim Coomer denies and for which Oltmann has never produced a recording. The claim spread through Trump-campaign lawyers and right-wing media. (Wikipedia: Joe Oltmann; Colorado Public Radio)

Coomer sued for defamation, naming the Trump campaign, Sidney Powell, Rudy Giuliani, FEC United, Oltmann and others. Colorado courts allowed the case to proceed, with a judge finding evidence rising to the "clear and convincing" standard that defendants published false statements with actual malice; an appeals court agreed the case could go forward. Coomer said he received death threats. (Colorado Newsline; 9News)

So when the user's brief says Oltmann "was sued because he falsely said the Dominion machines were messed up," the precise, documented version is: he originated the Coomer/Dominion conspiracy claim and became a defendant in Coomer's defamation lawsuit, which courts have allowed to move toward trial.

FEC United and the Grosse Pointe Yacht Club

To be precise about the sequence: FEC United was not founded at the Grosse Pointe Yacht Club. What is described locally is that Joe Oltmann spoke at the Yacht Club, after which an FEC United chapter formed in the area. Accounts connect the invitation to former school-board member Lisa Papas, whose son had worked for Oltmann in the Denver area. Mary Anne Brush's lawsuit references events at the Yacht Club (see the Brush account).

⚠ Needs local source  The Yacht Club appearance, the local FEC United chapter, and the Papas connection are community accounts. Brush's federal court filings are the most likely public record for what happened at the Yacht Club — a direct citation to that filing (or contemporaneous coverage) would move this from allegation to documented.

Ginny Jeup, January 6, and the four-vote seat

Ginny Jeup won her Grosse Pointe school-board seat by four votes after a recount against Tim Klepp — a 14-vote election-night margin that narrowed through certification and recount to a four-vote final result. (Grosse Pointe News)

Jeup attended the January 6, 2021 events at the U.S. Capitol. She has said she did not participate in or witness any violence. Her attendance, and how she has characterized it, became an issue in her school-board campaigns. (Michigan Public)

Framing note: "was at the insurrection and said she wasn't" overstates what's documented. The careful version: she was present in Washington at the Capitol on January 6 and has said she took no part in violence. If there is a specific on-record instance of her denying attendance, cite that statement directly rather than paraphrasing it. ⚠ Needs local source
