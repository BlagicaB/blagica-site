South vs North

Two high schools, one reputation gap

Grosse Pointe runs two public high schools. Understanding the difference in how they're perceived is necessary background for why the North health-clinic fight landed the way it did.

South and North

The district operates Grosse Pointe South and Grosse Pointe North high schools. South, the older of the two, is frequently treated — in local reputation and real-estate shorthand — as the more prestigious. North serves the northern communities and the Harper Woods side.

By the numbers, both are strong, but the gap is not only perception. U.S. News ranks South around #9 in Michigan (about 80% reading and 63% math proficiency, a 97% graduation rate), while North posts a 95% graduation rate and about 70% reading proficiency, with math proficiency (~38%) closer to the state average. (U.S. News: South; North) Fuller district numbers are on the Community page.

Honest note: both schools clear state averages comfortably, so this is a gap between "very good" and "excellent," not good-versus-bad. Rankings also vary by rater and year — figures above are the latest published by U.S. News, linked so you can verify.

The clinic and the funding question

The free youth health clinic that the board halted 4–3 in January 2023 was sited at Grosse Pointe North. The stated objection from the majority was that district tax dollars should not pay for it; the resolution paused work until an alternative that "wouldn't result in the use of tax dollars" could be found. (The Detroit News)

Supporters argued the funding objection did not hold up: construction (about $1 million) would come from the district's sinking fund, and operating costs would be covered by state and other grants — i.e., the money could lawfully be used for the clinic. Because a state grant required the clinic to be operating by fall 2023, the "pause" functioned as a cancellation once construction couldn't begin that winter. (C&G Newspapers; Michigan Public)

That a service aimed at all students ages 5–21 was blocked at North, on a funding rationale supporters called mistaken, is why many residents framed the decision in equity terms — the mechanics of the vote are documented in The Schools Saga.
