A documented history · Grosse Pointe, Michigan

Grosse Pointe, on the record

Some of Michigan's strongest public schools, deep civic roots — and, since about 2018, a run of school-board and election fights that drew statewide and national attention. This is a neutral, source-by-source account of both.

The Grosse Pointes are five small communities on Lake St. Clair with a genuinely high-performing school district (the numbers are here) and an unusually engaged citizenry. That engagement has two faces: the constructive one — booster groups, advocacy organizations, packed board meetings — and the combative one, which is what drew the headlines. Old money and new money. Longtime residents and newer, often more progressive families. A declining enrollment, a pandemic, a national wave of school-board politics, and a local billionaire who bought the town paper and then joined the school board.

This site documents all of it — the strengths and the disputes — because the way the pieces collided here is unusually well recorded: in board minutes, court dockets, campaign-finance filings and news coverage.

This site keeps a strict line between two kinds of statements:

  • Documented facts — votes, dates, filings, quotes — are linked to news reporting or public records.
  • Allegations, rumor and characterization — "pushed out," "threatened," "most likely because of politics" — are labeled as such and attributed to whoever is making the claim.

Where a claim is part of local memory but we could not yet find a primary source, it is marked ⚠ Needs local source so it can be sourced or corrected. See About & methodology.

The timeline

Each entry links to the fuller, sourced account. Dates are the date of the event, not of the reporting.

  1. 2018–19

    Enrollment decline forces a reckoning

    After 15 years of falling enrollment, the district studies closing schools and moving fifth grade. Community tension rises through the spring of 2019.

  2. Jun 2019

    Board votes to close two schools and reconfigure grades

    Poupard and Trombly are closed (5–2); grades are reconfigured (6–1), moving all fifth graders to the middle schools for 2020–21. Recall efforts target several trustees.

  3. Jan 2020

    Board president Brian Summerfield resigns

    After nearly seven years, Summerfield steps down citing the toll on his family, directly after the closure-and-reconfiguration year.

  4. 2020–21

    COVID, remote school, and the fight over reopening

    Buildings close in March 2020. How and when to return in person — and whether to require masks — becomes the organizing issue for a new political slate.

  5. Nov 2020

    A Grosse Pointe Woods resident at the center of the vote-count fight

    Monica Palmer, chairing the Wayne County Board of Canvassers, blocks then certifies then tries to rescind — after a call from President Trump.

  6. Nov 2022

    The board turns over; the Cotton era begins

    Sean Cotton tops the ballot and joins the board amid a national push by Moms for Liberty into local schools. Ginny Jeup wins a seat by four votes after a recount.

  7. Jan 2023

    The new majority halts the Grosse Pointe North health clinic

    A 4–3 vote stops a free youth clinic over a dispute about whether district funds could pay for it. A grant deadline turns the "pause" into a cancellation.

  8. 2023

    The district's communications director is cut — and later sues

    Mary Anne Brush, an ex–Grosse Pointe News reporter, loses her district job in board-driven budget cuts and files a federal wrongful-termination suit alleging political retaliation.

  9. Aug 2023

    Superintendent Jon Dean departs

    The board accepts Dean's "retirement" 4–2. A trustee says he was driven out; a later investigation records a witness quoting the board president about going after complainants next.

  10. Nov 2024

    Voters reject the Cotton-backed slate

    A four-candidate reform slate wins despite a heavily funded opposing campaign and billboards along I-94, shifting the board's majority.

  11. 2025

    The retaliation report is released

    The board votes to release the full outside investigation into a retaliation complaint, documenting how deep the divisions ran.

  12. 2025–26

    The next school-board election approaches

    With the board's balance again in play, residents are revisiting how the last eight years unfolded — which is why this record exists.
