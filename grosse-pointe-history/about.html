About & Methodology

How this site is built and sourced

This is a neutral, citation-first history. It exists because a community heading into another school-board election deserves to see the last eight years laid out in one place, plainly.

The two-tier rule

Every statement on this site is one of two kinds, and they are visually distinct:

  • Documented fact — a vote, date, filing, or direct quote — carries an inline link to news reporting or a public record. If it isn't linked, it isn't presented as fact.
  • Allegation / rumor / characterization — anything of the form "was pushed out," "was threatened," "most likely because of politics" — is labeled, attributed to whoever makes the claim, and never stated in the site's own voice.

Where a claim is part of local memory but lacks a primary source, it carries a ⚠ Needs local source badge. That badge is a to-do, not an endorsement.

Why the caution is a feature, not timidity

This project names real people and describes serious matters — elections, terminations, a defamation case, January 6. Neutral framing and hard sourcing are what make such a record credible and durable. A page that overstates — "she denied being at the insurrection" when what's documented is "she attended and says she saw no violence" — is both less fair and easier to dismiss. Precision is the point.

Corrections

If anything here is wrong — a name, a date, a vote count, a mischaracterization — it should be fixed fast and visibly. Set up a dedicated, low-friction channel (a protonmail-style address or a simple form) and list it here. Every correction that changes a factual claim should be noted so the record stays honest. ⚠ To add: corrections contact.

A note on attribution and anonymity

Anonymous civic publishing is a long-standing, protected tradition. But anonymity does not lower the accuracy bar — it raises it, because readers can't weigh the author's vantage point. So the standards above (source everything, label allegations, correct quickly) are the accountability here. Practically: keep author-identifying details out of the site and its hosting, use domain-registration privacy, and let the citations carry the weight.
